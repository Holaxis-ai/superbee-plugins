# Bounded research, reduction and continuation

1. **Inventory before searching.** Read the current questions and held evidence.
   Search identifiers, alternate literal names and prior corrections before acquiring
   another copy. Describe what is known, uncertain and worth deciding.
2. **Choose a useful investigation.** State a bounded question and why its result
   could change understanding. Include ordinary alternatives. Exploration is valid;
   do not invent a binary hypothesis for every interview or qualitative inquiry.
3. **Preserve evidence honestly.** Record actual representation, locator, edition,
   capture/inspection scope and access limits. Acquisition and review are separate.
   Preserve permissible original bytes and transformations through an appropriate
   companion when needed; installing this recipe performs neither capture nor OCR. For retained bytes,
   put an exact local locator and its path base in the Source, including which
   original or derivative was actually inspected. A later reader should not need
   a synthesis or capture manifest to guess where the original lives.
4. **Separate observation from interpretation.** Faithful transcription supports
   what a source says. Assess real-world claims separately. Names, proximity,
   chronology and wording alone do not establish identity or causation.
5. **Connect evidence deliberately.** Explain how each support/challenge applies
   to the claim's subject and conditions. Check shared provenance before counting
   corroboration. Preserve uncertainty when apparently conflicting sources concern
   different periods, populations, scenarios or decision stages. Unique Source IDs
   or independence-group labels do not prove independent samples. Record known
   overlap and use derivation links only for identified inputs; unknown lineage
   remains a limit rather than an invented edge.
6. **Reduce before continuing.** Review actual inputs and results; state what
   changed and what did not. A completed agent assignment is not an answered question.
   Append bounded negatives to the existing question instead of generating an empty
   result document per search. Keep one current frontier and link retained history.
7. **Correct without erasing.** Retain earlier observations and claims. Link the
   successor, explain the changed scope/evidence and inspect known dependents. A
   weakened support path triggers review; independent support may remain valid.
8. **Stop or park when appropriate.** State the missing evidence and a concrete
   revisit trigger. Repeating an unchanged inaccessible archive is not progress.
   No fixed duration or document count substitutes for expected information value.

Keep evidence and draft Markdown outside the bundle root; `--dir` names that root
itself. The own-study example uses a `.superbee` child so narrative and temporary
files do not accidentally become indexed records. A short synthesis remains the
human entrypoint; the graph supports a later audit, not a requirement to read every
record for every answer. For a complete body use `doc read <id> --body-out -`.
JSON `--offset 0` starts paging; follow `range.next_offset` and pass the first
`head_version` with `--expected-version` until the final page. Do not equate the
first page with the complete document.

## Parallel work

Partition by independent evidence or discriminating questions, with named scope
and output responsibility. Avoid concurrent edits to one frontier document.
Reconcile the actual observations, contradictions, duplicate origins and unfinished
lanes before selecting another round. More agents and more documents are not
evidence of better research. Tasks/rounds are optional coordination companions.

## Consumer handoff

A synthesis links its evidence-backed claims and open questions, states applicable
scope and review limits, and distinguishes a current interpretation from a pinned
snapshot. Publication, human authorization, privacy and access control remain
separate. A source-backed remark does not authorize making it policy.
